A useful rule is that most -ir stem-changing verbs keep the stem change in the gerund, while verbs with a vowel before the ending often take -yendo. For example, leer becomes leyendo and dormir becomes durmiendo.
Handy pattern

    Vowel stem + gerund ending: leer → leyendo, creer → creyendo, oír → oyendo, traer → trayendo.

Stem change preserved: pedir → pidiendo, sentir → sintiendo, servir → sirviendo.

Special cases: ir → yendo and poder → pudiendo.

Verb	Meaning	Common uses	Regional note
encender	to turn on / light	lights, electrical devices, fires	Most common in Spain
prender	to turn on / start	lights, car (prender el coche)	More common in Latin America
arrancar	to start	car engine specifically	Used for starting cars
Examples

    Lights: Enciende la luz / Apaga la luz (turn on/off the light)

Car: Arrancar el coche or Prender el coche (start the car) / Apagar el coche (turn off the car)

Electrical appliance: Encender el televisor / Apagar el televisor
Key point: In Spain, encender is the standard verb for "turn on" (lights, devices), while apagar covers all "turn off" situations. In Latin America, prender is often used instead of encende
