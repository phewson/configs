
* First aid kit

Assorted plasters, including a few large ones.
Blister treatment and/or hydrocolloid blister pads. Compeed
Sterile gauze pads and non-adherent dressings in a couple of sizes.
Cling film and electrical tape
Field dressings
Medical tape that sticks in the wet; zinc-oxide tape or similar is especially useful outdoors.
A self-adhering elastic bandage such as Vet Wrap for compression and support.
Sam Splint
Triangular bandage.
Nitrile gloves, at least 2 pairs.
Antiseptic wipes.
Pain relief you can take safely.
Your usual prescription meds and a spare inhaler.
An antihistamine for allergic reactions.
Foil blanket.
Tweezers and small trauma scissors.
Waterproof notebook and pencil or phone notes.
Emergency whistle and headtorch, especially if you may be out late.

* Boat repair kit

For a sea kayak, I’d carry:

    Waterproof tape or rescue tape.
    Epoxy putty stick.
    A small tube of marine sealant suited to your boat material.
    A few zip ties.
    Sandpaper or abrasive pad.
    Spare hatch gasket or hatch-specific repair material if relevant.
    A short note in the kit saying what material your kayak is made from and what repair product works on it.
    self-amalgamating tape;
a marine sealant;
epoxy putty only if it is explicitly suitable for polyethylene or for the sort of repair you intend;
a small emergency hatch cover or at least a hatch gasket solution if your boat takes a common size;
a bit of abrasive pad/sandpaper for ashore repairs.
- 3M Scotch 23 or another 3M self-amalgamating tape.
- Tesa 4600 if available.
- Raychem / TE Connectivity self-amalgamating tape.
- Scapa 2501/2504 or similar PIB tape.
- Advance Tapes AT87 as a practical UK option
